Why also test what happens after a click? The weekly ranking deliberately never clicks anything, so it shows the state before you have made any choice. But many sites load very little until you accept, and others keep contacting trackers even after you reject, so a count taken before the click can be misleading in both directions. The two tests above press one consent button, or block a public list, in separate runs and publish the results next to the ranking without changing it. They help answer a simple question: does my choice at the banner change what the site does? They are limited by what our detector can find, and a number there is a measured fact, not a finding of wrongdoing.
