How to read it

  • Presence, not volume. “{{ st.leader }} is on {{ st.sites }} sites” says that one of its services was contacted, not how much, nor what it does with the data.
  • Figures are minimums. The classification list is limited, and tag managers, consent tools and paywalls are not counted as tracking, so the real presence can only be larger.
  • Measured before any consent, from {{ vantage }}. Sites that show their consent banner only to European visitors look worse here than they do to a visitor in Europe.
  • Not a market share and not a verdict. The index describes how concentrated the presence on these news sites is. It does not say anything about legality or intent. See the method.

How to read this

  • Reach is a count of sites, not of readers, requests or data, and it describes the moment before the visitor interacts. It says nothing about what the operator does with a request, nor whether it is lawful.
  • Operators are named as in our classification list. Subsidiaries and brands of the same group are not merged (for example “Microsoft” and “Microsoft (Xandr)” are separate rows), so a group's real reach can be higher than its largest row. Our list is limited, so every figure is a minimum.
  • Inferred marks operators whose every entry was identified from general knowledge of the vendor and not verified one by one (see the limits).
  • “On its sites it ran scripts on…” counts the sites where that kind of activity was seen, taken from the measured request log; what these things mean on your computer.
  • Only sites measured with high or medium confidence are in the denominator. Sites that refused the automated browser are listed under not measured; they may well contact more.
  • The download entities.csv has these rows. Each site's page lists the operators behind its own count.
